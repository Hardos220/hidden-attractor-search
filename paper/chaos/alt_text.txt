Alternative text for the figures and tables of the manuscript
"Searching for hidden attractors as a rare-event problem: An adaptive strategy portfolio with a worst-case guarantee"
(Maksim Elisov). Items are numbered as in the compiled manuscript.

FIGURES

Figure 1. Grid of twelve three-dimensional phase portraits of the hidden chaotic attractors of the level-A benchmark systems, one panel per system, with equilibria drawn as red crosses and, for the LE1 system, the line of equilibria drawn as a red segment through the attractor.

Figure 2. Chua circuit with parameter set II in three panels: the large limit cycle with the three equilibria at its center, an enlarged view of the two small chaotic attractors around the stable foci, and a colored basin map in the plane z equals zero in which the chaotic basins are narrow strips.

Figure 3. Two panels for the synthetic family D2: escape time against the relative distance to the basin boundary on logarithmic axes with the exact escape-time law as a line, and the radial coordinate of the splitting-chain seeds contracting below the basin boundary over successive levels.

Figure 4. Basin map of the Jiang NF I e map with gray escaping and blue chaotic-basin regions, overlaid with the seeds of the splitting chains at level zero as open circles and at level one as black triangles on the thin tongues of the basin.

Figure 5. Seven panels of detection probability against the number of integrations for the rare targets of levels A and B, with curves for uniform sampling, differential evolution, splitting, active learning, and the portfolio, and a dotted line at the 0.9 level.

Figure 6. Two log-log panels of detection cost against basin measure for the synthetic families D2 and D1, with fitted scaling exponents written in each panel and a gray line for the theoretical cost of uniform sampling.

Figure 7. Three panels for the coupling-born hidden attractor of two coupled Chua circuits: circuit 1 on the large cycle, circuit 2 hopping between the regions of the two former hidden attractors shown in gray, and the largest Lyapunov exponent against the coupling strength.

Figure 8. Chart of the gain of the portfolio over uniform sampling for each problem in integrations, integrator steps, and wall time on a logarithmic axis, with the problems grouped into escaping and bounded systems and a dashed line at gain one.

Figure 9. Chua circuit with parameter set I: the two symmetric hidden chaotic attractors with the stable equilibrium, the saddle-foci, and their unstable manifolds in three dimensions, and a colored basin map in the plane z equals zero.

Figure 10. Lyapunov exponent of the Jafari 2026 system against the parameter a3 in the narrow chaotic window near the boundedness limit, with a dashed line marking where trajectories begin to escape to infinity.

Figure 11. Four phase-and-basin portraits of two-dimensional maps (Huynh 2019 and three Jiang cases) with light gray escaping regions, dark blue chaotic basins, white cycle basins, orange fixed-point basins, and black attractors.

Figure 12. Four-dimensional Li and Sprott system: a projection of the torus and the two symmetric strange attractors, and a basin section in which the torus occupies the background and the strange attractors form spiral tongues.

Figure 13. Two panels on numerical transient chaos in Chua circuit I: the fraction of copies remaining on the hidden chaotic set against time for two integration steps, and the basin fractions against the integration step.

Figure 14. Two panels: the largest Lyapunov exponent over consecutive observation windows for all benchmark targets, and a long lifetime check of eight trajectory copies for the Lorenz transient, the Rabinovich set, and the Jafari chaos.

Figure 15. Heat map of gain factors relative to uniform sampling, with benchmark systems in rows and method variants in columns, colored by the logarithm of the gain, with the values printed in the cells.

Figure 16. Two panels for real systems in enlarged regions: basin measure of the hidden target against the enlargement factor compared with the inverse-cube expectation, and the gains of the portfolio and of splitting against basin measure.

Figure 17. Gain of the portfolio and of splitting on the synthetic family D2 against basin measure down to 1e-7, with a variant that redraws coordinates and with reduced integration steps that restore the gain at the two smallest basin measures.

Figure 18. Log-log plot of detection cost against the dimension of the state space for the family D2 at fixed basin measure, with uniform sampling near the theoretical line and the portfolio and splitting far below it from dimension six on.

Figure 19. Median distance from long-transient nontarget points to the target against the upper-set fraction for seven problems; decreasing curves, drawn solid, go with a splitting gain, and increasing ones, drawn dashed, with its absence.
