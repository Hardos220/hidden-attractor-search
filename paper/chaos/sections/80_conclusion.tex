\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusion}

The portfolio of splitting driven by transient duration, active learning of basin boundaries, and a uniform share finds rare hidden attractors $10^3$--$10^4$ times faster than uniform sampling on coupled copies of systems with hidden attractors, changes the order of growth of the cost from $p^{-1}$ to $p^{-0.45}$ where the transient signal is present on all scales, and is 3--7 times faster on the rare hidden attractors of the benchmark that have such a signal, while it is guaranteed never to be much worse than uniform sampling in the number of integrations. Where trajectories are bounded, as in the Chua circuits, the gain carries over to wall time. The source of the gain is the transient duration as a reaction coordinate: a single stagger-and-step chain on the same coordinate is as strong where the signal is present, which makes it a natural further arm of the portfolio, and the uniform share keeps the guarantee where the coordinate is uninformative. The cases without gain (deceptive coordinates, discrete escape times, long boundaries, the time-resolution limit, and escape so fast that a uniform point is cheap) delineate the domain of applicability.

Formulating the search for hidden attractors as rare-event detection makes search methods comparable: the cost $N_{0.9}$ relative to uniform sampling is a single number that any method can report, and uniform sampling itself is a reference whose cost $2.3/p$ is known in advance and is within a factor of about 2.6 of the best label-only algorithm. The benchmark, the identification and verification protocol, and the synthetic families with exactly known basin measure are independent of our method and can serve as a common test bed for other localization methods.

Beyond the method, the work leaves two tools and one object. The hiddenness measure $h_\varepsilon$ grades hidden attractors by the scale at which the equilibria cease to lead to them; the verification protocol catches both transient chaos mistaken for an attractor and an attractor destroyed by a coarse integration step; and the enumeration of regimes with that protocol revealed a hidden chaotic attractor of two coupled Chua circuits that the single circuit does not have.
