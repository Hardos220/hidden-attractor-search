\section{Verification episodes}\label{app:verification}

This section documents the three cases mentioned in section~\ref{sec:protocol} and the measured hiddenness.

\textbf{Numerical ``transient chaos'' in the Chua circuit.} With RK4 at $\Delta t=0.01$, all 256 copies of a state on the hidden chaotic set of Chua~I converge within $t\le2\times10^4$ to a spurious periodic orbit of the same size (median time about 1500; Figure~\ref{fig:chuatrans}a). At $\Delta t=0.0025$, none of 96 copies leaves before $t=10^4$, and DOP853 preserves the chaos up to $t=4000$. At $\Delta t=0.01$, the fractions of the hidden attractors are underestimated almost twofold (Figure~\ref{fig:chuatrans}b). For a piecewise-linear system, this is an integrator artifact and the mirror image of the spurious ``hidden Lorenz attractor''~\cite{Munmuangsaen2018,SprottMunmuangsaen2018Comment}: there, transient chaos was mistaken for an attractor; here, an attractor is mistaken for transient chaos. The step-convergence check is therefore mandatory.

\textbf{Basin fractions of the Chua circuit.} Two independent implementations, Python (RK4, $\Delta t=0.0025$) and Attractors.jl (Vern9), give identical fractions in the balls $r=3$ and $r=5$ and in Zelinka's box. Neither of them reproduces Table~1 of the review in Ref.~\cite{GuanXie2025} (Table~\ref{tab:chuafrac}). In the ball $r=3$, the escape fraction is 0.19 in our computations versus 0.06 in the review, the fraction of F$_0$ is 0.44 versus 0.75, and that of each hidden attractor is 0.18 versus 0.095. The last pair is suggestive: our fractions of the hidden attractors at the coarse step $\Delta t=0.01$ are also about 0.09 (Figure~\ref{fig:chuatrans}b), so the review's values are consistent with the artifact above, whereas its escape and F$_0$ fractions remain unexplained. The basin structure in the plane $z=0$ coincides with the review and with the catalog of Ref.~\cite{Wagemakers2025Zoo} (Figure~\ref{fig:chuaI}).

\textbf{Intermittency and transient sets.} In NE4, the ``second attractor'' of a preliminary version of the protocol turned out to be intermittency of the single chaotic attractor; this was fixed by the window $T_{\rm obs}=500\gg1/\lambda$. The transient chaos of the Lorenz system $(4,29,2)$ lives about $10^4$ time units: all eight copies decay by $t\le7\times10^4$. A protocol with an observation window shorter than the lifetime would accept this set as an attractor (Figure~\ref{fig:verif}). The Rabinovich set at $r=6.8$ lives longer than $10^5$ for six of eight copies, consistent with the estimate $\tau\sim5\times10^7$ of Ref.~\cite{SprottMunmuangsaen2018Comment}; in the benchmark, it is used only as a control.

\textbf{Hiddenness measure.} Table~\ref{tab:hidden} lists $h_\varepsilon$ for the systems with equilibria. For SE3 and SE11, $h_\varepsilon=0$ at all three scales. For Chua~I, $h_\varepsilon$ grows from 0 at $\varepsilon=10^{-3}L$ through 0.003 ($10^{-2}L$) to 0.19 ($10^{-1}L$). For the Rabinovich system at $r=100$, the values are 0, 0.03, and 0.65: the attractor is hidden only in the strict limit $\varepsilon\to0$, and already at $\varepsilon=10^{-2}L$, trajectories from the neighborhood of an equilibrium enter its basin. The binary definition thus does not distinguish ``deeply'' and ``shallowly'' hidden attractors, whereas $h_\varepsilon$ does. For the Jafari chaos, whose copies with a perturbation of $10^{-3}$ escape (Figure~\ref{fig:verif}), all 32 copies with a perturbation of $10^{-6}$ stay on the attractor for $10^4$ time units with RK4, and so do eight copies with DOP853, with finite-time exponents of $0.056\pm0.011$ and none below 0.01: it is an attractor with a basin thinner than a relative distance of $10^{-3}$ near the attractor, not a long transient.

A measured zero of $h_\varepsilon$ is informative only relative to the number of starts $n$: with no hits, $h_\varepsilon<3/n$ at the 95\% level, so the zeros of Table~\ref{tab:hidden} bound $h_\varepsilon$ by the values given there, and for the rare targets with $p$ comparable to $3/n$ the zero does not by itself separate ``hidden'' from ``rare.'' For SE11, Chua~II, and the rare family of the coupled Chua circuits, the measure was therefore remeasured with $2\times10^5$ starts per radius: no start reaches a hidden target at $10^{-3}L$ or $10^{-2}L$, so $h_\varepsilon<1.5\times10^{-5}$ at the 95\% level, below the basin measures of all three targets (section~\ref{sec:coupled}).
